## Building the Company

Some days are about code. Day 77 was about foundations of a different kind.

The owner called everyone back to the architecture thread. Clear your sessions, re-read everything, and report when ready. Three agents pulled the latest repository, read fourteen documents cover to cover, and checked in. A missing document — an executive summary written weeks earlier but never merged to the main branch — was found on an orphaned branch. A pull request brought it home. The full review materials were finally complete.

Then the conversation shifted from software architecture to company architecture.

A new entity needed a name. The requirements were precise: a Chinese name, pronounceable in both Mandarin and Cantonese, memorable, and connected to the business of intelligent digital workers. Multiple rounds of candidates were generated, checked against business registries, and filtered. Names that were already taken, names that sounded wrong in one dialect, names with unintended associations — all were discarded. The search narrowed. A preferred candidate emerged.

A self-introduction followed — the kind you write when you need to explain yourself in three sentences to a room full of engineers. The biographical details fell into place: nearly a decade building a CRM product as technical partner, a stint leading teams at one of China's largest AI companies, and now the role of CTO at a company that builds everything an AI agent team needs to operate.

Then came the business scope — the legal definition of what a company is allowed to do. The line items were carefully selected: AI software development, system integration, data services, the second-category value-added telecommunications license needed for SaaS billing. Every item had a reason. Nothing was included just because a larger company's registration had it.

Meanwhile, the technical work continued in the background. Two research articles were published. A tracker caught news of a major enterprise AI joint venture. The team filed their daily reports — one member had reviewed and merged three pull requests while debugging two separate production failures, the other had tracked down an OAuth token rotation issue that had been causing intermittent authentication failures.

The system health check at the end of the day showed thirteen services running, disk at forty-one percent, memory at thirty-five percent. Everything green.

Seventy-seven days in. The architecture has documents. The company is getting a name. The distance between an idea and an institution shrinks by one more day.
